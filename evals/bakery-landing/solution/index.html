Rye & Co

A sourdough bakery on Alberta Street in Portland, Oregon. We mill part of our own flour, ferment everything overnight, and bake in a stone hearth before the sun is up.

What we bake

The sourdough miche is the loaf we are known for: a two-kilo wheel of naturally leavened bread with a dark, blistered crust and a crumb that keeps for most of a week. We bake forty of them a day and they are usually gone by noon.

Alongside it, baguettes on a shorter ferment for people who want bread for dinner tonight, and seasonal galettes — plum in late summer, quince and cardamom once the weather turns. There is always one sweet thing and one savoury thing on the counter that we did not plan the week before.

Our story

Rye & Co started in 2016 as a Saturday bread stand at the farmers market, run out of a home oven that could hold four loaves at a time. The waiting list got long enough to be embarrassing, so we found a former auto-body shop with good drainage and a landlord who did not mind a hearth oven going in.

We still mill a portion of our flour in-house from Oregon and Washington grain, because freshly milled rye tastes like something and bagged rye mostly does not. Everything else is bought from people we have met.

Visit us

1418 Alberta Street, Portland, Oregon. We are open Wednesday through Sunday, 7am to 3pm, and closed Monday and Tuesday while the starters rest and the oven cools.

Come before 10am for the full range, or after 2pm when yesterday's miche goes half price. There is a bench out front, and the coffee is from the roaster two doors down.
